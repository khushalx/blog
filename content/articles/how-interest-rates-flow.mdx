## A policy rate is a starting point

A change in interest rates does not affect every household and business at once. The impact depends on how borrowing is priced, when contracts reset, and whether lenders pass the change through.

A household with a floating-rate loan may face a different adjustment from one with a fixed-rate mortgage. A company refinancing soon can be more exposed than one with long-dated funding. Timing is part of the economics.

## Follow the decisions

Higher borrowing costs can make a planned factory, home purchase or inventory build less attractive. But the effect depends on expected income and returns as well as financing costs. A business with strong demand may still invest.

There are also savers on the other side of the transaction. Higher deposit income can support their spending, while borrowers reduce theirs. The net result depends on the balance of these responses and how they evolve.

## Avoid a single-cause story

Inflation, currency movements, fiscal policy and credit conditions interact. A rate change is one input into a larger system, and the effects can appear with delays.
