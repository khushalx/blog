## Bigger is not the same as more valuable

Revenue growth describes an increase in sales. It says little about the cash generated after paying operating expenses and funding the assets needed to support those sales.

Consider a hypothetical company that adds ₹100 crore of annual revenue but needs ₹80 crore of additional capital to deliver it. If the expansion produces only ₹4 crore of sustainable annual after-tax operating profit, its incremental return is 5%. These figures are invented to isolate the logic.

## The opportunity cost of capital

Whether that expansion creates value depends on the return required for its risk and the timing of cash flows. If investors require more than the project can earn, expansion can destroy value even while reported revenue grows.

<ResearchCallout>
  The relevant question is not simply how fast the company can grow. It is how
  much value it creates for each unit of capital retained and reinvested.
</ResearchCallout>

## Read the cash-flow statement alongside the story

Check whether receivables and inventory are increasing faster than sales, whether expansion spending is recurring, and whether acquisitions account for much of the growth. Each can change the meaning of the headline number.

These are starting questions, not automatic warning signs. Working capital may rise ahead of a seasonal sales period, and new capacity may need time to mature. The task is to connect the financial statements with the actual operating cycle.
