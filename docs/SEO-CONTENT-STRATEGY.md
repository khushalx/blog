# Content strategy for The Long View

## Position and readers

The Long View is a student-led, independent publication about business economics, valuation, markets and technology. Its first published article uses Tesla’s 2023 filing to examine revenue growth and cash generation. The strongest audience is a reader who wants to understand *why* a business or financial metric behaves as it does: students, analysts, finance professionals and curious long-term investors. The publication should earn trust through transparent method, original calculations and precise sourcing, not a claim of professional advisory authority.

## Four content pillars and clusters

1. **Company economics:** distribution, pricing power, customer retention, unit economics and reinvestment. Each future company report can link to a short explainer of the operating metric it uses.
2. **Valuation and capital allocation:** P/E assumptions, ROCE, reinvestment, cash conversion and incremental returns. The published revenue-and-cash essay can anchor follow-up pieces on these questions.
3. **Markets and macro transmission:** interest rates, bank funding costs, working capital and business investment. Ground each essay in a concrete company or industry example.
4. **Technology and finance:** how AI spending, software adoption and financial data systems change company economics. Tie claims to filings and measurable business effects.

Avoid launching indexable topic pages for every label. Add a topic landing page only after multiple substantial pieces share a genuine reader question; give that page an original introduction and curated links.

## Distinctive formats

- **Research note:** business model → industry structure → evidence table → competing explanations → valuation scenarios → falsifiable watch list → dated sources. Explain the strongest counterargument.
- **Metric essay:** start with a misconception, show a small labelled calculation, then show where the metric breaks down in a real business.
- **Update note:** revisit a prior hypothesis after a new annual report or earnings release; state what changed and what did not.
- **Source trail:** link filings and mark which statements are reported facts, calculations or interpretation. Use `sources` frontmatter for structured references when useful.

9. **Article:** How an interest-rate change reaches a bank, a borrower and a consumer company at different speeds.
10. **Article:** The difference between a company's good economics and a good share-price outcome, with scenario arithmetic.

These are briefs, not published claims about current company performance. Verify the latest available filings and dates before writing. Maintain a two-articles-per-week rhythm only if evidence and editing quality hold; one substantial research note per month can anchor related explainers.

## Internal links and updates

Each future company note should link to two or three concepts needed to understand it. Each concept essay should link back to a real company application, with descriptive anchor text. Related sections provide a light default but should be manually curated when a stronger connection exists. Avoid circular or unrelated links. Keep published slugs stable; if a public slug changes, add a host-level 301/308 redirect.

Before publishing: confirm the reader question, original contribution, primary sources, calculation definitions, uncertainties, counterarguments, title/description, one H1, headings, image rights/alt, related links and demo/draft flags. After a material update: verify sources again, explain what changed, set `updatedAt`, check links and re-read the conclusion. Thin outlines, rewritten press releases and unsourced AI summaries should remain drafts rather than indexable pages.
